About

A focused research program on machine-readable claims.

RISU Institute studies bounded, load-bearing questions in automated systems: what a machine-readable claim means, what evidence supports it, when another system may rely on it, and when its effects are actually over.

Research program

Current work spans three connected threads: semantic identity and preservation; evidence qualification and transport; and reliance, finality, and closure. The threads connect the work without enlarging the claims made by any individual artifact.

Research method

Several of these questions have long histories in legal and institutional reasoning, including authority, revocation, reliance, recognition, absence, finality, and evidentiary status. RISU uses those concepts as design vocabulary when they expose technical distinctions that software otherwise collapses. The resulting artifacts specify technical contracts rather than legal conclusions.

Existing standards, formal literature, and deployed systems define the starting point. New abstractions are introduced only where a bounded operational gap remains after that comparison.

Claims are then narrowed until they can be tested through adversarial cases, constructive controls, live-system commissioning, or archived evidence.

Publication discipline

Published claims are scoped to the evidence that supports them. Experimental status, assumptions, trust boundaries, failed mechanisms, and unresolved questions remain visible in the public record. Supporting artifacts are published for inspection where practical.

Direct observation, inference, and normative proposal are kept separate. RISU research publications are collected under RISU Technical Notes.

Organization

RISU Institute is a research initiative of RISU LLC. Public source and experimental artifacts are maintained through the RISU Research GitHub organization.
