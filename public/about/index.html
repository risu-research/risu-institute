About

A small institution by design.

RISU Institute is a research initiative of RISU LLC focused on narrow problems in machine-readable systems whose consequences can propagate across interfaces, protocols, and automated decisions.

Research program

RISU studies semantic integrity from interface to action: what must remain explicit when software exposes, translates, composes, or acts on machine-readable claims.

The current program examines missing semantic identity, unsupported semantic gain, silent semantic loss, and downstream reliance on insufficiently warranted premises. These categories connect the work without enlarging the claims made by any individual artifact.

Approach

We begin with a specific interoperability, legal, technical, or institutional problem and reduce it to the smallest claim that can be tested.

Existing standards and systems are treated as constraints, not blank space. New layers are added only when the surviving problem cannot be represented cleanly by what already exists.

Where possible, claims are made executable through frozen inputs, adversarial cases, reproducible controls, constructive references, or recorded real-source evidence.

Publication discipline

Experimental work is labeled experimental. A passing internal test is not presented as independent adoption. A carrier is not presented as a guarantee. Semantic discovery is not presented as authorization, legal entitlement, trust, or successful execution.

Direct observation, inference, and normative proposal are kept separate so that later review can narrow or reject a claim without obscuring the underlying evidence.

Current work

Current public artifacts include Appeal, the HTTP→MCP Method-Inference Soundness Profile, the OpenAPI→MCP Problem-Semantics Preservation Profile, and Negative Result Warrant.

Organization

RISU Institute is a research initiative of RISU LLC. Public source and experimental artifacts are maintained through the RISU Research GitHub organization.
