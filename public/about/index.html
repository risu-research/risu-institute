About

A focused research program on semantic assurance for machine action.

RISU Institute studies load bearing questions that appear when machine readable claims cross boundaries and become operational: what they mean, what evidence supports them, when another system may rely on them, and whether a consequential action is actually determined.

Research program

Current work spans three connected threads: semantic identity and preservation; evidence qualification and transport; and reliance, finality, and consequence closure. The threads describe a progression from naming machine readable meaning, to preserving and qualifying evidence, to deciding when that meaning is strong enough to support reliance or action. Each artifact remains bounded to its own formal and empirical record.

Research method

Several of these distinctions have long histories in legal and institutional reasoning, including authority, revocation, reliance, recognition, absence, finality, notice, supersession, and evidentiary status. RISU treats those concepts as sources of technical distinctions, not as legal conclusions. When a concept exposes a failure mode that software compresses into a boolean, a loosely typed status, or an implicit assumption, the research asks whether that distinction can be expressed as a carrier neutral semantic object, verification condition, or replayable evidence contract.

Existing standards, formal literature, and deployed systems define the starting point. A new abstraction is introduced only where a bounded operational gap remains after that comparison. The abstraction is then pressured with adversarial cases, constructive controls, source conformance, or real system commissioning according to the claim being made.

Model relative reasoning, source preservation, and operative system evidence are kept distinct. This makes it possible to strengthen one link without silently treating that result as proof of another.

Publication discipline

Published claims are scoped to the evidence that supports them. Experimental status, assumptions, trust boundaries, failed mechanisms, and unresolved questions remain visible in the public record. Supporting artifacts are published for inspection where practical.

Direct observation, inference, formal consequence, and normative proposal are kept separate. RISU research publications are collected under RISU Technical Notes.

Organization

RISU Institute is a research initiative of RISU LLC. Public source and experimental artifacts are maintained through the RISU Research GitHub organization.
