Current research program

Semantic integrity across protocol boundaries.

Research program · 2026

Missing. Invented. Lost.

What happens to machine-readable meaning when systems expose, translate, or connect semantics across protocol boundaries?

Current RISU work examines three bounded patterns: a useful shared semantic identity may be missing, translation may introduce meaning unsupported by its source, or translation may silently discard meaning already present. The grouping is organizational; each artifact retains its own evidence, scope, and claim limits.

Method

Make the claim smaller until it becomes hard to break.

01

Start narrow

Prefer the smallest reusable semantic or mechanism over a new platform.

02

Freeze the semantic contract

Define what must survive, and what must not be inferred, before evaluating implementations.

03

Test executable claims

Use adversarial fixtures, frozen inputs, and reproducible controls where code can falsify the claim.

04

Publish what survives

Separate direct observation from inference, and do not turn an experiment into a standard before the evidence earns that claim.
