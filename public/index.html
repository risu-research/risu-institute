Meaning, evidence,

automated systems.

RISU Institute studies what machine-readable claims mean, what evidence supports them, and when another system may rely on them as they cross interfaces, change representation, move across authority boundaries, or persist through changes in operational state.

Research questions are narrowed into technical contracts, executable tests, and public artifacts that can be inspected against the claims they support.

Reliance Before Closure

When can one machine rely on a claim before the surrounding workflow has finished?

Some workflows remain open after the uncertainty relevant to a particular downstream decision has already become bounded. Reliance Before Closure defines an evidence-qualified interface for that case. Upstream qualification establishes the relevant future boundary and action effect under an explicit trust profile; a provider-blind consumer checks whether the action remains within the certified stability margin. The profile was commissioned on Kubernetes, including a preserved failed mechanism, and the archived release supports offline reconstruction of the recorded result.

Machine-readable claims across boundaries.

What must remain explicit as claims cross interfaces, change representation, or outlive the process that produced them?

Automated systems increasingly depend on claims that cross interfaces, change representation, or survive changes in authority and operational state. RISU studies the obligations that allow those claims to be interpreted, supported, and relied on without silently gaining meaning, authority, or certainty.
