RISU Institute studies the load bearing questions that appear when machine readable claims cross interfaces and become operational: what they mean, what evidence supports them, when another system may rely on them, and whether the consequence of a concrete action is actually determined.

Research is expressed as bounded semantic contracts, adversarial verification, real system commissioning, and public artifacts that can be inspected against the claims they support.

RISU Technical Note 2026-03 · August 2026

Consequence Closure

At the moment a machine acts, is the specified consequence already determined by the evidence and operative state?

Consequence Closure makes residual consequence dependence an explicit assurance target. At a declared action cut, relational counterexamples expose what can still change the result. The formal core characterizes inclusion minimal semantic obligations, keeps those obligations separate from realizable Establishments and Routes, and defines P0, P1, and P2 preservation levels for carrying closure claims across source abstractions. A frozen differential against the official Cedar 4.12.0 source oracle confirmed exact P1 on the declared bounded surface. Separate Linux, SQLite, filesystem, OAuthLib, and Keycloak commissioning exercises test operative and evidence semantics at real action cuts.

Research program

From semantic identity to operative consequence.

Three connected research threads · 2026

Machine claims that survive contact with action.

What must remain explicit as a machine readable claim becomes evidence, reliance, and action?

RISU studies a chain of load bearing questions that software often treats separately: semantic identity, preservation across representations, evidence qualification, reliance under unresolved effects, and consequence determinacy at the action boundary. Each project targets a bounded failure mode. Together they form a research program for making machine claims explicit enough to verify, transport, and act on without silently gaining meaning, authority, or certainty.
