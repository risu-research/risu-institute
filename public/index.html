Research program · 2026

Missing. Invented. Lost. Unwarranted.

What must remain explicit when software exposes, translates, composes, or acts on machine-readable meaning?

Current RISU work isolates four bounded failure modes: a useful shared semantic identity may be missing; translation may invent meaning unsupported by its source; translation may silently discard meaning already present; or a downstream action may rely on a negative premise without sufficient bound evidence. Each artifact retains its own evidence, scope, and claim limits.

Experimental executable reference profile · 2026

Negative Result Warrant

When a bounded query returns zero, what evidence is sufficient for a downstream operation to use that exact negative premise?

The reference profile binds provider-reported negative evidence to the exact query proposition, carries it across MCP, validates it at the receiver, and gates a deterministic downstream effect. In the recorded Algolia contrast, an ordinary zero-result observation remained UNKNOWN; a separate verification observation with explicit source-reported scope evidence became WARRANTED_ZERO.

Method

Make the claim smaller until the evidence can carry it.

01

Start narrow

Prefer the smallest reusable semantic or mechanism over a new platform.

02

Freeze the claim boundary

Define what may be asserted, what must survive, and what must not be inferred before evaluating implementations.

03

Test executable claims

Use adversarial fixtures, frozen inputs, real-source contrasts, and reproducible controls where code can falsify the claim.

04

Publish what survives

Separate observation from inference, preserve negative results, and do not turn an experiment into a standard before the evidence earns that claim.
