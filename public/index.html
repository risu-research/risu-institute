Research program · 2026

Missing. Invented. Lost. Unwarranted. Laundered.

What must remain explicit when software exposes, translates, composes, or acts on machine-readable meaning?

Current RISU work isolates five bounded failure modes: a useful shared semantic identity may be missing; translation may invent meaning unsupported by its source; translation may silently discard meaning already present; a downstream operation may rely on a negative premise without sufficient bound evidence; or an evidentiary state may cross system boundaries and acquire a stronger status without reconstructed support. Each artifact retains its own evidence, scope, and claim limits.

Evidence should not strengthen in transit.

LAUNDERED · Experimental conformance system · 2026

ClosureProbe

When a system turns “we did not find it” into “it does not exist,” where did that stronger claim first appear?

ClosureProbe receiver-reconstructs supplied negative evidence and traces its anchored state across observable source, adapter, protocol, client, model-facing, and claim boundaries. It localizes the earliest boundary where that evidentiary basis is lost, substituted, or strengthened without reconstructed support. rc3 freezes five producer-specific profiles plus one controlled generic contract and a 50-case adversarial/control corpus; third-party client measurements are the next empirical phase and are not yet claimed.

Method

Make the claim smaller until the evidence can carry it.

01

Start narrow

Prefer the smallest reusable semantic or mechanism over a new platform.

02

Freeze the claim boundary

Define what may be asserted, what must survive, and what must not be inferred before evaluating implementations.

03

Test executable claims

Use adversarial fixtures, frozen inputs, real-source contrasts, and reproducible controls where code can falsify the claim.

04

Publish what survives

Separate observation from inference, preserve negative results, and do not turn an experiment into a standard before the evidence earns that claim.
