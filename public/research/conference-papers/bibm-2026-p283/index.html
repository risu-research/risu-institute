Research overview

Clinical-trial data-sharing portals may remain online after a sponsor moves its request process elsewhere. A working page and a plausible transition notice do not necessarily mean that the access policy underneath belongs to the named sponsor. This study traces historical ClinicalStudyDataRequest (CSDR) pages and identifies three cases in which the displayed sponsor and the body of the policy refer to different companies.

The project began with an exploratory inspection of 14 registered trials across five sponsors, examining whether older data listings and current request routes remained findable. The later, narrower policy-attribution analysis is the primary reproducible result. These distinct samples are not combined into a prevalence estimate.

Historical evidence

Fifteen exact Common Crawl WARC response records, with recorded archive coordinates and cryptographic hashes, support the principal historical comparisons. Extracted policy paragraphs match in full for Lilly versus Boehringer Ingelheim (27/27), Bayer versus Sanofi (25/25), and ONO versus UCB (21/21). Six selected other-sponsor comparison controls yield no complete policy-body match (0/6). Dates are pinned; the captures are not all contemporaneous.

The finding concerns attribution of policy text and possible differences in how data-access conditions are understood. It does not measure how often such errors occur across the industry, whether a particular research application was rejected, or what source-code defect caused the mismatch.

Reproduction and source boundaries

The public GitHub package provides the WARC coordinates, hashes, comparison scripts, tests, and expected results. The frozen Zenodo v1.0.0 software archive supplies a citable version. Original third-party page texts and WARC payloads are retrieved from their public archive rather than redistributed by RISU.

The original HTTP responses for the October 2026 14-trial route observations, and the complete original response set for 19 exploratory sponsor-page comparisons, were not retained for identical independent replay. Their recorded observations are distinguished from the independently re-executable WARC evidence in the repository's claim–evidence map.
