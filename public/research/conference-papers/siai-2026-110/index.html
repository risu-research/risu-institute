Which notice starts the clock?

A Medicaid appeal can pass through several decisions before a member requests a state fair hearing. The date that starts the filing period matters. Louisiana's review of a dental plan found two policies counting the hearing window from the initial benefit decision. A contract amendment instead specified 120 calendar days after the plan's appeal-resolution notice.

That difference points to a wider problem in corrective action. An auditor identifies a flawed instruction, then someone must find the right version of the rule and the document that needs to change. The SIAI research follows this path through federal requirements, plan contracts and audit evidence.

From an audit finding to a specific correction

Louisiana's 2025 compliance review covered the plan's 2024 operations. The grievance-and-appeal standard contained six unmet elements. Three examples show how the relevant source changes the work to be done.

The cases call for different records: revised member instructions, a corrected filing period, or a policy tied to its governing clause. Following each one also means checking which procedural stage the text covers. A grievance, an appeal and a state fair hearing have different steps.

When a search finds the neighboring rule

Source retrieval is part of this work. In three frozen tests using Nevada Medicaid audit reports, stage-filtered TF–IDF selected the assigned requirement for 8 of 8 SilverSummit action queries, 7 of 8 SilverSummit finding queries, and 3 of 7 Molina findings. The shift in wording and contractor changed how reliably the right item appeared first.

The experiments also removed the assigned requirement from the available sources. A nearby clause could then rise to the top of the ranking, sometimes with a wider score gap. The experiment makes a practical point: the source supporting a correction needs its own identity and provenance, beyond a plausible match in a search result.

Following the record beyond the presentation

Further source work traced the original Louisiana contract and Amendments 1–13, separating changes to grievance handling, appeals and hearings. It also compared federal provisions with the plan's contract language on continued benefits. The resulting source comparison connects each issue to dated documents and specific clauses.

Two publicly released 2025 member handbooks add another view. Both direct members to request an appeal by phone. Their acknowledgment sections, however, still begin with an appeal received “in writing.” Reading those instructions together shows where a member might need a clearer account of what happens after calling. The handbook record preserves the versions, page locations and PDF fingerprints.
