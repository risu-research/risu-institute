Research

RISU studies how machine readable claims acquire operational force as they move from meaning to evidence to reliance and action. The program is organized into three connected threads that preserve the boundary of each individual research claim.

Research thread

Reliance, finality, and consequence closure.

Consequence assurance · RISU Technical Note 2026-03 · 2026

Consequence Closure

At the moment a machine acts, is the specified consequence already determined by the evidence and operative state?

Consequence Closure defines consequence determinacy at a declared action cut. Relational counterexamples preserve what remains material, a materiality hypergraph characterizes inclusion minimal semantic obligation sets, and finite CEGIS either returns one such set or exposes a declared vocabulary boundary. Obligations remain separate from realizable Establishments and Routes. P0, P1, and P2 state what a source front end must preserve before closure, obligation, or Route claims can be exported. A frozen differential against the official Cedar 4.12.0 source oracle confirmed exact P1 on the declared bounded surface, while separate commissioning exercises test operative and evidence semantics across Linux, SQLite, filesystem, OAuthLib, and Keycloak.

Reliance · RISU Technical Note 2026-02 · 2026

Reliance Before Closure

When can one machine rely on a claim before the broader workflow has finished?

Reliance Before Closure defines an evidence-to-reliance interface for claim-relative decisions under unresolved effects. Provider-specific qualification establishes a future boundary under an explicit trust profile, a verified stability certificate carries a decision margin, and a proposed action is separately bounded by its possible movement toward the verdict's flip boundary. The provider-blind relying function consumes only those bound semantic objects. Kubernetes commissioning included an initial mechanism that remained REVIEW. In a later trust-scoped execution, the relying function returned SUPPORTED before the subsequent Pod creation succeeded and before broader Deployment closure.

Finality and closure · RISU Technical Note 2026-01 · 2026

Bounded Agent Closure

After an autonomous agent is disabled, what proves that the consequences attributable to it have actually reached a terminal, stable state?

Bounded Agent Closure distinguishes administrative retirement from operational closure. Under the explicit RISU_AGENT_CLOSURE_V0 evidence profile, its deterministic verifier follows evidence-backed residual consequences transitively from the retiring root, evaluates postconditions across authority, execution, commitments, and operational state, and requires stable convergence before issuing the bounded claim CLOSED.

Evidence transport · Experimental conformance system · 2026

ClosureProbe

Where does a query-relative negative conclusion first become stronger than the evidence that crossed the boundary?

ClosureProbe is a receiver-anchored executable conformance system for negative-evidence integrity across observable agentic toolchains. It reconstructs root evidence from supplied source material, binds source context and the exact negative proposition, propagates evidence anchoring through the trace, and localizes the earliest unsupported strengthening, substitution, scope drift, or profile change. It does not decide whether nothing exists; it tests whether the supplied evidence still justified saying so after the observed boundaries.

Projection assurance · RISU Technical Note 2026-04 · 2026

Projection Assurance

When a source operation becomes an agent-facing tool, do the distinctions that determine its consequence survive the projection and still govern the effect?

Projection Assurance evaluates consequence preservation across agent-facing interfaces relative to a declared source consequence. It separates source-target correspondence, consequence discrimination, operative placement, exact realization, and source-family coverage. Retained GitHub and Azure projections show that surface visibility and surface omission are both unreliable preservation rules; a prospectively frozen PayPal holdout tests the same framework on idempotency semantics. The reported evaluation is preserved in a frozen v0.2.0 Evaluation Capsule, while Consequence-Preserving Projections v0.7.0 is the current implementation.
