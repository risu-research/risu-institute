Reliance Before Closure

A workflow can still be open even after the uncertainty relevant to one particular decision has become small enough for another machine to act on that decision.

Core idea: Reliance Before Closure separates workflow completion from decision readiness. It asks whether real evidence has bounded the unresolved futures relevant to one claim, whether that claim remains stable across those futures, and whether a proposed action fits inside the certified margin without changing the claim's verdict.

The gap before closure

Many systems wait for a workflow-wide completion signal before allowing downstream use. That is safe but can be unnecessarily coarse. A deployment may still be converging, a controller may still have work to do, or a distributed process may still be open even though the futures that could change one specific claim have already been constrained.

The opposite shortcut is worse: seeing a favorable snapshot and acting as if the unfinished future no longer matters. Reliance Before Closure sits between those two extremes. It does not require global completion, but it also does not treat a current value as stable merely because it looks good now.

One claim, not the whole workflow

The profile is claim-relative. It asks whether a particular verdict remains stable over the unresolved futures that matter to that verdict. Other parts of the workflow may remain incomplete or irrelevant to that question.

This distinction is what makes early reliance possible without pretending that the system is globally finished. The result is narrow by design: a relying party receives support for one bounded semantic claim under one declared boundary and trust profile.

Four obligations have to line up

The project keeps four questions separate because success on one does not answer the others.

Future boundary
Which unresolved paths are still admissible, which have been excluded by evidence, and which remain explicit trust assumptions?
Verdict stability
Given that qualified future boundary, how far can the claim move before its verdict flips?
Action effect
How much can the proposed action itself worsen the same claim, including qualified action-induced future expansion?
Relying identity
Do the certificate, action bound, and relying party agree on the exact claim, semantic profile, unit, boundary identity, and trust-profile identity?

A positive result requires all four. The arithmetic comes last.

Evidence and assumptions stay different

The difficult part is not comparing two integers. It is deciding what future uncertainty those integers are allowed to summarize.

Reliance Before Closure records paths that were excluded by operational evidence separately from paths accepted as trust assumptions. A positive boundary may therefore be trust-scoped without pretending that an assumed absence was empirically proved.

If an unknown path could still worsen the claim and has not been evidenced away or explicitly accepted under the named trust profile, the system does not manufacture a positive margin. It requalifies instead.

A current bound is not automatically a stable bound

The recorded mutable-bound control makes this concrete. A ResourceQuota hard limit may be actively enforced at the current moment, but if the evaluated actor can relax that quota, the current number does not justify a positive future-stability certificate for that boundary.

The control therefore returns REQUALIFY. Current enforcement answers what is true now. Reliance-grade stability asks what remains true across the admissible future.

The stability margin

For the frozen scalar profile THRESHOLD_SLACK_V0, a verified stability certificate carries a nonnegative margin M. The margin is decision-sufficient only after the boundary is proven and the claim, semantic profile, unit, boundary identity, and trust-profile identity are fixed.

The margin means that the claim's relevant quantity can worsen by a bounded amount before the verdict changes. It is not a universal safety number and it has no meaning outside the certificate's semantic and trust bindings.

The action is qualified separately

The proposed action receives its own bound, Cₐ. That bound covers the action's direct claim-worsening effect plus any qualified future expansion caused by the action itself.

This separation matters. A stable claim does not automatically make every action safe to take against it. The relying decision compares a verified stability margin with a separately proven action effect.

The provider-blind relying function

Once provider-specific evidence has been turned into a verified stability certificate and a qualified action bound, the final rely() function does not need Kubernetes-specific logic or another provider's internal model.

It checks exact semantic identity, requires a proven boundary and stable certificate, requires a proven action bound scoped to post-action admissible futures, and then applies the profile rule:

stable SATISFIED: SUPPORTED iff Cₐ ≤ M stable VIOLATED: SUPPORTED iff Cₐ < M

The violated case is strict because consuming the entire violation margin reaches the threshold equality and changes the verdict.

Matching numbers are not enough

A counterfactual Inspector case uses M = 1 and Cₐ = 1, so the arithmetic fits. But the relying context rejects the trust profile accepted by the certificate and action bound.

The result is NOT_APPLICABLE, not SUPPORTED. The profile refuses to let two mutually matching artifacts silently choose assumptions for the relying party.

SUPPORTED is not permission and not a reservation

The relying function returns only SUPPORTED, REQUALIFY, or NOT_APPLICABLE. Every output explicitly says that authorization and reservation were not inferred.

The recorded concurrency case shows why. Two one-slot actions can each be semantically within the same one-slot stability margin when evaluated independently. That does not reserve the slot twice. At commit time the authoritative Kubernetes quota admitted one action and rejected the other.

Reliance therefore answers a semantic applicability question. The authoritative system still controls authorization, coordination, admission, and commit success.

The Kubernetes commissioning

The project commissioned the interface against one local Kubernetes scenario rather than stopping at fixtures. At the reliance point, the claim-relevant rollout creation condition had become stable under an explicitly trust-scoped boundary while the broader Deployment completion condition was still false.

The archived run derived M = 1 POD_SLOT. A server-normalized standalone Pod CREATE was separately qualified at Cₐ = 1 POD_SLOT. The provider-blind consumer returned SUPPORTED, the actual Pod CREATE succeeded, and broader Deployment completion arrived later.

What the commissioning actually demonstrated

operational evidence ↓ future-boundary qualification ↓ stability margin M ↓ separately qualified action bound Cₐ ↓ provider-blind rely() ↓ actual commit ↓ later broader workflow closure

The useful result is the separation itself: in the recorded scenario, machine-consumable evidence supported a claim-specific reliance decision before workflow-wide completion. The project preserved the evidence, assumption, semantic, boundary, trust, and action-effect bindings needed to make that handoff reviewable.

Negative controls make the boundary clearer

The recorded and counterfactual cases are useful because they show several superficially attractive shortcuts failing for different reasons.

Mutable hard bound
REQUALIFY. Current enforcement is not future stability when an admissible actor can relax the bound.
Trust-profile mismatch
NOT_APPLICABLE. Matching arithmetic cannot override a relying party's rejected trust assumptions.
Replacement reserve
REQUALIFY. If an additional adverse replacement path is included, the effective margin falls from one slot to zero and the proposed one-slot action no longer fits.
Concurrent one-slot actions
Each can be SUPPORTED semantically, while the authoritative quota still admits only one. Proof is not reservation.

How this differs from waiting for closure

Broader closure asks whether the workflow has reached its own terminal condition. Reliance Before Closure asks whether a smaller, claim-specific decision boundary has already become good enough for a downstream machine to consume.

Waiting for closure remains valid when the narrower boundary cannot be qualified. The profile does not force early action. It gives a machine-readable way to distinguish cases where waiting is still necessary from cases where the evidence already supports a bounded reliance decision.

How this differs from a safety filter

Formal safety filters, runtime verification, relative information completeness, coordination-avoidance results, assume/guarantee reasoning, attestation architectures, and action-governance systems already cover important neighboring ideas. Reliance Before Closure does not claim to have invented those foundations.

Its narrower engineering contribution is the executable seam between heterogeneous operational evidence, an explicitly qualified unresolved-future boundary, a claim-specific stability result, a separately qualified action-effect bound, and a provider-blind relying party.

The Inspector

Reliance Inspector v0.4.0 exposes the recorded final commissioning case, recorded negative controls, and generated counterfactuals. It shows the decision trace, boundary status, evidence-backed exclusions, explicit trust assumptions, stability margin, action bound, identity diagnostics, timing, and nonclaims.

The hosted surface is intentionally canonical-only. Full evidence ZIP verification and arbitrary semantic-object evaluation remain local capabilities of the archived v0.4.0 release. The hosted app.js, styles, and canonical case data are frozen copies, while deployment-only navigation and scope disclosure remain separate.

Open Reliance Inspector

Three levels of reproducibility

Recorded evidence
The frozen commissioning ZIP can be verified offline with Python 3 and no Kubernetes cluster. The verifier rechecks the archive manifest, boundary accounting, trust assumptions, raw Kubernetes objects, margin and action derivations, relying result, final quota state, quota-failure absence, and lead arithmetic.
Semantic core
The zero-dependency Python consumer, canonical fixtures, conformance checks, and exhaustive integer oracle can be run independently. A TypeScript implementation is retained as a second implementation.
Fresh commissioning
A local kind cluster can rerun the commissioning harness through boundary accounting, margin derivation, server-side action normalization, action-bound derivation, provider-blind reliance, commit, and broader closure.

Result

Reliance Before Closure makes a narrower completion boundary consumable by another machine without pretending the broader workflow is finished. The relying party gets a bounded semantic result only after evidence, assumptions, claim identity, trust identity, stability, and action effect agree.

The recorded Kubernetes commissioning supplies an existence result for that architecture: one trust-scoped operational trace reached SUPPORTED, committed the qualified action, and only later reached broader Deployment closure.

Where the profile stops

SUPPORTED is not authorization, a reservation, a transaction, an admission decision, or a guarantee that a concurrent commit will succeed. The minimal reference also does not authenticate certificates or action bounds; verified authenticity is a caller-side precondition.

The Kubernetes result is one scenario-conditioned commissioning trace, not evidence of production prevalence or a universal early-action window. The positive boundary accepted explicit trust assumptions. Those assumptions remain part of the claim rather than being silently promoted to evidence.
